# Presentation: what the player sees and hears

The game is about one decision, taken again and again: one more spin, or deliver? The screen's job is to make that decision readable at a glance and make the result feel big. Art style is open; this document sets what must be shown, not how it's drawn.

## The screen during a round

| Area | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Wheels**, in a row across the middle | Every wedge on every wheel, face up. Above each wheel: its chance to spoil the load right now, as a percentage and a colored ring. |
| **Truck**, below the wheels | The cargo loaded so far, in the order it landed. Tarps hang on the side; crates stack on top. |
| **Quota line**, painted on the truck bed | How full the load is against the round's quota. When the load is close, the line glows. This is the temptation, so it has to be visible. |
| **Load value**, on the truck | What the load would pay if delivered now, updated after every spin. |
| **Fuel gauge** | Spins left this round. |
| **Round bar**, at the top | Score so far against the target, and the inspection's rule if there is one. |
| **Items**, in a row at the top | In the order they apply. The player drags to reorder. |
| **Consumables**, at the side | Ready to use when a spin goes wrong. |

Two buttons carry the game: **Spin** (on each wheel) and **Deliver** (on the truck).

## Rules the presentation follows

- **Open information.** Nothing the player needs is hidden. They can see every wedge, the spoil chance of every wheel, and the exact payout of delivering now. The uncertainty is only in where the pointer stops.
- **Wedges that would spoil the load are marked** on every wheel as soon as the truck holds their value. The player never has to count.
- **Payouts play out step by step,** in the order the rules apply: cargo points, mult for load size, color groups and runs, each item in turn, crates, the quota bonus, the full-truck bonus. Each step shows its own number. Speed is adjustable, and a click skips to the total.
- **A spoiled load is shown, not hidden.** The cargo tips off the truck, and the value lost is shown for a moment. Losing has to sting enough that the next decision matters.
- **Saves are loud.** When a tarp, a nudge or a respin saves a load, the game makes a moment of it. These are the stories players tell.

## Feel

- **The spin.** The wheel ticks past wedges and slows down. As it slows, the wedge under the pointer lights up: green for safe, red for a duplicate. The last few ticks are where the tension lives, so they get the time.
- **Near misses.** When the pointer stops next to a wedge that would have spoiled the load, the game marks it. This is what makes the nudge consumable feel fair when it's the other way round.
- **Escalation.** As a load grows, the truck sinks on its springs, the music adds layers, and the payout numbers get bigger and faster. A full truck is the biggest moment in the game.
- **Sound** carries the risk: the tick of the wheel, a rising tone as the load nears the quota, a hard stop when it spoils.

## Accessibility

- **Color is never the only signal.** Each cargo color has its own shape and symbol.
- **Animation speed** can be set, down to instant.
- **Odds are always numbers,** as well as colors and rings.
- **No time pressure.** Nothing in the game is timed.

## Desktop first

- **Mouse:** everything is one click. Hovering a wedge, item or wheel explains it.
- **Keyboard:** number keys spin wheels, Space delivers.
- **Controller and handheld** come later; the layout above fits a 16:9 screen with no scrolling.
